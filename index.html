How to read it

The most useful column is the transmission level, which says how far a virus is known to travel between people rather than how dangerous it is. A level 2 virus can be lethal; a level 4b virus can be mild. They answer different questions.

  • Level 2 (147 species) — limited onward transmission between humans
  • Level 3 (32 species) — longer chains of transmission between humans
  • Level 4a (36 species) — sustained human-to-human transmission, with an animal reservoir
  • Level 4b (24 species) — sustained human-to-human transmission, no animal reservoir needed

Vectors and routes are recorded separately: a virus can have a documented arthropod vector and also documented person-to-person routes. A blank is an absence of recorded evidence, not evidence of absence.

What this data does not say

  • 32 of 239 records carry no reference sequence accession.
  • 0 have no transmission route recorded, which means the source found no evidence to record, not that no route exists.
  • 0 have no host category recorded.
  • 206 have no SpillOver risk score, because SpillOver ranks a different set of viruses; 33 do.
  • 48 carry at least one cell the source marks with an asterisk. The mark appears on both yes and no values and the dataset does not define it, so each value is read as it stands and the mark is kept alongside it.
  • 65 records sit on coordinates shared with at least one other record, so the first-report locations are not plotted on a map.

The catalogue records human infection, not disease burden, severity or current circulation. A species being listed says someone documented a human infection and published it. For anything consequential, read the cited literature.
